OSU IT Service Desk

Corvallis, OR · 2024 to present

About the role

I work at the Oregon State University IT Service Desk in Corvallis, the first point of contact for students, faculty, and staff seeking technical assistance. The position covers phone triage, ticket management, and any technical support needs.

A shift often ranges from five to seven hours. I begin by reviewing the tickets assigned to me during my previous shift, following up with users who are waiting on a response, and checking for any outages before joining the phone queue. In between calls, I continue working through open tickets. A typical shift involves more than ten tickets and two to three calls per hour, increasing to four or five at the start of a term when enrollment questions are common.

What the work requires

The job is less about knowing all the answers and more about being able to think on your feet, communicate effectively, and provide a good user experience under pressure. Patience and empathy are crucial, since users often face frustrating technical issues and already tried multiple solutions before reaching us. Research matters almost as much, because I need to understand an unfamiliar issue quickly while identifying the underlying cause by asking key questions.

When something falls outside what we can resolve, the work becomes finding a workaround or escalating the issue correctly to a higher-level support team. Recognizing when and where to escalate is crucial for maintaining a high level of service. Our knowledge base articles are also a crucial resource, an internal search system holding documentation on known issues, departments, and services across the university. It is the first place any technician need to look up on almost every call. When I find an article that is outdated or missing information, I pass the correction or update to my coworker that maintains it so we can keep it current as a team.

Skills demonstrated

ServiceNow · Phone triage · Technical Troubleshooting

What I have acquired over the year

Most of what I have learned over the year is about the distance between user expectations and the reality of technical limitations. I have become more adept at managing difficult conversations and providing clear, actionable guidance to users over the phone or by connecting to their computer. Asking the right questions has become a key skill in diagnosing issues efficiently. However, the goal is not only to resolve issues but to show the reason why the issues occur.

I also see the same problems recurring, which has helped me identify patterns and improve my approach to handling similar situations in the future. They usually come from widely used software rather than unique or obscure issues. Therefore, I focus on understanding the root causes and the design decisions behind them that never got feedback on.

This changed how I approach problem-solving and my own work. My background is in human-computer interaction, which has helped me better understand user needs, especially when I get the chance to support people daily to get clear sense of what confuses them. When I contributed to Pencil2D, I applied these insights and navigated through the application the way a new user would and made notes where I encountered confusion. This experience has enhanced my usability testing skill and led to the fixes I implemented.

Takeaways

The work at the IT Service Desk has been a valuable experience, providing me with a deeper understanding of technical support and user needs. It has honed my problem-solving skills, improved my communication abilities, and reinforced the importance of empathy in customer service. These skills are transferable to many areas of work and life, making this role an essential part of my professional development.
